> **In brief:**
> 1. Orange is France's best mobile network for the fifteenth consecutive year according to ARCEP (91% of perfect calls), with a marked lead in rural areas and on trains.
> 2. Bouygues Telecom tops the nPerf 2026 barometer (latency, web browsing, 5G speed) and ties with Orange in dense urban areas.
> 3. Free Mobile closes the quality rankings but offers the widest 5G coverage; SFR is improving and takes third place.

Two sources are the reference for judging a mobile network in France. **ARCEP's annual measurement campaign**, carried out by field teams with more than one million measurements between June and August, and the **nPerf barometer**, aggregated from tests launched by users of the app. The two do not measure the same thing and do not give exactly the same ranking. Here is what they say about the four networks in 2026.

## The ranking in one table

| Operator | Perfect calls (ARCEP 2025) | Rural areas | Long-distance trains | nPerf 2026 score | 5G speed (3.5 GHz) | Latency |
|----------|----------------------------|-------------|----------------------|------------------|--------------------|---------|
| Orange | 91% | 83% | 74% of calls maintained | 3rd | 227 Mbit/s | Behind Bouygues |
| Bouygues Telecom | 88% | Behind Orange | Under 60% | 1st, 95,684 points | 231 Mbit/s | 30.15 ms, the best |
| SFR | 87% | Behind Orange | Under 60% | 2nd | 224 Mbit/s | Behind Bouygues |
| Free Mobile | 84% | 76% | 61% | 4th, 86,183 points | 198 Mbit/s | Weaker in rural areas |

## Orange: the reference network, especially outside cities

ARCEP crowns **Orange** best mobile network for the **fifteenth consecutive year**. Nationally, **91% of calls** are judged perfect. The gap with competitors widens where the network is hardest to hold: **83% in rural areas** (against 76% for Free) and **74% of calls maintained on long-distance trains** (against 61% for Free and under 60% for Bouygues Telecom and SFR).

On speeds, nPerf gives it the **best average download speed** in 2026, 128.91 Mbit/s, all technologies combined, and 227 Mbit/s in 5G on the 3.5 GHz band. Orange ranks third in the overall nPerf score, behind Bouygues Telecom and SFR, which reflects latency and web browsing below Bouygues in the everyday use of the app's users.

## Bouygues Telecom: the smoothest day to day

**Bouygues Telecom** wins the **nPerf 2026 barometer** with an overall score of **95,684 points**. It dominates on **latency** (30.15 ms) and **web browsing** (75.05%), the two indicators that weigh most on the feeling of smoothness, and shows the **best 5G speed**, 231 Mbit/s on the 3.5 GHz band. In ARCEP measurements, it ranks second with 88% of perfect calls and **joint first with Orange in dense urban areas**, above 95% call quality.

Its weakness is that of every network other than Orange: transport. Under 60% of calls maintained on long-distance trains.

## SFR: the year's improver

**SFR** improves in the ARCEP 2025 measurements and takes **third place** with **87% of perfect calls**, one point behind Bouygues Telecom. It ranks **second in the nPerf barometer** and shows 224 Mbit/s in 5G. On text message delivery, all operators are close, between 93% and 96% within ten seconds. SFR is also, according to ARCEP's satisfaction observatory, the operator that best resolves mobile problems (88%).

## Free Mobile: last on quality, first on 5G reach

**Free Mobile** closes both rankings: **84% of perfect calls** at ARCEP, **86,183 points** at nPerf, with weaker results on browsing and latency in rural areas and **76% call quality** in the countryside. ARCEP notes, however, that Free and SFR are improving in 2025.

Free compensates with a different coverage strategy: it deployed **5G on the 700 MHz band** on most of its masts. This band carries far and penetrates buildings well, but offers less speed than the 3.5 GHz band. The result, according to nPerf: **Free has the widest 5G coverage**, with 198 Mbit/s average speed, against 231 Mbit/s for Bouygues Telecom. Free also remains strong on video streaming, the most common use of mobile data. It is the trade-off assumed by the cheapest operator on the market.

## ARCEP or nPerf: which measurement to trust?

Both. **ARCEP** measures in controlled conditions, on defined routes (cities, countryside, roads, trains, metro), with the same handsets: it is the reference for comparing the **intrinsic quality** of networks, especially voice. **nPerf** aggregates tests launched spontaneously by users, wherever they are and with their own phone: it is a good reflection of the **perceived experience** on data, biased towards populated areas and savvy users. When the two converge (Orange in rural areas, Bouygues in cities, Free behind on latency), the signal is solid.

## How to choose your network

- **Check coverage at your address** on ARCEP's map, monreseaumobile.arcep.fr, which shows each operator's 4G and 5G coverage building by building.
- **Rural area or train journeys**: Orange, or Sosh for the same network without a contract.
- **City, online gaming, video calls**: Bouygues Telecom or B&You, best latency.
- **Budget and wide 5G**: Free Mobile, whose 5G coverage makes up for a lower peak speed.
- **Test before committing**: every low-cost operator is contract-free, a month's trial costs under 20 euros.

## Method and sources

Data from the [ARCEP 2025 measurement campaign on mobile service quality](https://www.arcep.fr/actualites/actualites-et-communiques/detail/n/qualite-des-services-mobiles-en-metropole-201125.html) and the [nPerf barometer of mobile connections in France](https://media.nperf.com/files/publications/FR/FR-Barometer-mobile-connections-nPerf-2025_7885.pdf), 2026 edition. The figures quoted are those published by these two bodies; no measurement was carried out by the editorial team.
