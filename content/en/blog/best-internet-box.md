> **In brief:**
> 1. The Freebox Pop S (24.99 euros per month, fixed price, no contract) is the best internet box of 2026 for most households.
> 2. The real comparison criterion is the cost over 24 months: most offers rise by 7 to 10 euros in month 13, except the Freebox Pop S and the RED Box.
> 3. At the very top of the range, the Freebox Ultra and the Livebox Max fight for first place; the Freebox Ultra wins on included services and the absence of a contract.

Comparing internet boxes in 2026 is no longer about speed: every fibre offer from the major operators exceeds 1 Gbit/s and the difference between 5 and 8 Gbit/s cannot be felt in any home use. The criteria that matter are the **real price over 24 months**, whether or not there is a **commitment**, the **Wi-Fi** generation, the **TV and streaming services included** and the quality of **customer service**. Here is the comparison built on public price lists recorded on 13 September 2026.

## 1. Freebox Pop S: the best box for most households

The **Freebox Pop S** ticks every box for the standard household. At **24.99 euros per month**, it is the only fibre box on the market whose price does not change after twelve months. It carries **Wi-Fi 7**, fibre speeds up to **5 Gbit/s** and a **Wi-Fi repeater included on request**, which competitors charge for or reserve for their higher ranges. Like every Freebox, it is **contract-free**.

Over 24 months, the bill reaches 599.76 euros, against 719.76 euros for a Bbox Fit that offers neither Wi-Fi 7 nor a repeater, and 827.76 euros for a standard Freebox Pop with its 4K TV Player. That gap justifies first place.

## 2. Freebox Ultra: the most complete at the top of the range

The **Freebox Ultra** is the most complete box on the market in September 2026. At 49.99 euros per month then 59.99 euros, it includes **Netflix, Disney+ and Prime Video** at no extra cost, **quad-band Wi-Fi 7**, **symmetrical 8 Gbit/s** and a repeater. A household already paying for these three subscriptions separately saves more than the price difference with an entry-level box.

Its direct rival, Orange's **Livebox Max Fibre**, costs 47.99 euros then 57.99 euros with a 12-month commitment and 49 euros of set-up fees (waived until 7 October 2026). It offers 200 channels, a second decoder and a 5 euro monthly discount, cumulative, on each of four partner platforms (Netflix, Disney+, Ligue 1+, HBO Max). The Livebox Max wins for those attached to the Orange network and its three repeaters; the Freebox Ultra wins on included content and the freedom to leave.

## 3. Freebox Ultra Essentiel: the power without the platforms

The **Freebox Ultra Essentiel** (39.99 euros then 49.99 euros, no contract) keeps the Ultra's 8 Gbit/s speeds and quad-band Wi-Fi 7, with 340 TV channels via the Free 4K TV Player, but without Netflix, Disney+ and Prime Video. It is the right choice for a highly connected household that already has its streaming subscriptions or does not want them.

## 4. RED Box Fibre: the cheapest, no frills

At **22.99 euros per month with no contract and no increase**, the **RED Box Fibre** is the most economical offer at list price. It is limited to 1 Gbit/s, Wi-Fi 6 and optional TV. For a student or a second home, that is enough. For a household with a television and several connected devices, the Freebox Pop S justifies its extra 2 euros.

## 5. Bbox Must and Bbox Ultym: the Bouygues network at a conditional price

Bouygues Telecom applies discounts tied to holding one of its mobile plans: the **Bbox Ultym** costs 45.99 euros with a Bouygues mobile, 52.99 euros without. The **Bbox Must** costs 36.99 euros then 43.99 euros. Both impose a 12-month commitment. The Ultym stands out with certified tri-band Wi-Fi 7. Note that the ARCEP 2025 observatory reports a decline for Bouygues Telecom on fixed-line problem resolution (78%).

## 6. SFR Fibre: the widest range, a single contract-free model

SFR splits its fibre into four tiers, from the **Box Starter** at 27.99 euros to the **Box Premium** at 45.99 euros (8 Gbit/s, Wi-Fi 7). Only the Premium is contract-free. The **Power S** at 29.99 euros is worth a look for remote work thanks to its "guaranteed internet" service. SFR nevertheless remains the operator with the lowest overall satisfaction score from ARCEP (7.5 out of 10).

## The criteria that really make the difference

- **The cost over 24 months**, not the introductory price. Adding 12 months at the promotional rate and 12 months at the full rate reveals gaps of 100 to 200 euros between offers displayed at the same price.
- **The commitment**. A contract-free box can be cancelled the following month; a box on a 12-month contract means paying the remaining months or exit fees.
- **Wi-Fi 7 and repeaters**. In a home of more than 70 m², the box's own Wi-Fi is not enough. An included repeater is worth 50 to 100 euros.
- **Included services**. Three streaming platforms are worth 25 to 35 euros per month at list price.
- **Customer service**. The ARCEP 2025 observatory gives the edge to Orange (90% of fixed-line problems resolved) and to Free on overall satisfaction (8.1 out of 10).

## Method and sources

Prices recorded on 13 September 2026 on the operators' sites and specialist comparison sites (Ariase, Echos du Net, Cable Review, La Fibre Optique). Satisfaction scores from the [ARCEP customer satisfaction observatory](https://www.arcep.fr/cartes-et-donnees/nos-publications-chiffrees/satisfaction-client/observatoire-de-la-satisfaction-client-edition-2026.html). Limited-time promotions change every month: check the price at the time of subscribing.
