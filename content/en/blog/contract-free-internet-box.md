> **In brief:**
> 1. Free is the only operator whose every box is contract-free; RED by SFR and the SFR Box Premium complete the list.
> 2. The Freebox Pop S (24.99 euros per month, fixed price) is the best contract-free box of 2026; the RED Box Fibre (22.99 euros) is the cheapest.
> 3. Contract-free does not mean fee-free: count set-up and cancellation fees, which range from 0 to 59 euros depending on the operator.

A **contract-free** internet box can be cancelled at any time, without paying the remaining months or giving a legitimate reason. In 2026 this is no longer a niche: the operator that imposed the model, **Free**, only sells contract-free offers, and SFR's low-cost brands have followed. Orange and Bouygues Telecom still tie their box subscribers to 12 months. Here are the contract-free offers available on 13 September 2026 and what they are really worth.

## Why Free dominates the contract-free market

Free built its reputation on the absence of contracts and keeps it across its whole range, from the **Freebox Révolution Light** to the **Freebox Ultra**. Three points make the difference against the low-cost brands that have joined it on this ground.

The **fixed price of the Freebox Pop S**, 24.99 euros per month with no increase, is unique on the fibre market. Where the RED Box Fibre offers the same price stability, it tops out at 1 Gbit/s in Wi-Fi 6; the Pop S goes up to 5 Gbit/s in Wi-Fi 7 with a repeater included on request.

**TV is included**: the Oqee app on the Pop S, a 4K HDR Player with more than 300 channels on the Pop, 340 channels on the Ultra Essentiel. At RED, TV is a paid option.

**Mobile discounts**: the Free 2 euro plan drops to 0 euro, and the 5G+ 350 GB and Free Max plans get up to 10 euros off per month per line for Freebox subscribers. A family with four lines saves 40 euros per month, which effectively makes the box free.

## RED by SFR: the cheapest, provided you want nothing else

The **RED Box Fibre** at 22.99 euros per month, contract-free and with no increase, is the most economical offer at list price. It suits a student flat, a second home or a household that watches TV through streaming platforms. Adding the TV option or a repeater brings the bill back up to the level of a better-equipped Freebox Pop S.

The **RED Box 5G** at 24.99 euros per month is the alternative for homes not connected to fibre: 1.1 Gbit/s download, 150 Mbit/s upload, with no work or technician.

## SFR Box Premium: SFR's contract-free top of the range

The only contract-free box in the SFR range, the **Box Premium** at 45.99 euros per month offers 8 Gbit/s and Wi-Fi 7 at a stable price. It compares with the Freebox Ultra Essentiel (39.99 euros then 49.99 euros), which costs 6 euros less in the first year and 4 euros more afterwards, and with the Freebox Ultra, which adds Netflix, Disney+ and Prime Video for 4 euros more in the first year.

## What "contract-free" does not cover

- **Set-up fees**: they exist at every operator, with or without a contract, and are often waived in promotions. Check the amount before subscribing.
- **Cancellation fees**: charged on leaving, they are sometimes refunded by the new operator on presentation of the invoice. Free, Bouygues Telecom and SFR offer this refund under conditions.
- **Returning equipment**: the box and decoders must be sent back within the stated period, or the unreturned equipment is billed.
- **The month-13 increase**: it also applies to contract-free offers (Freebox Pop, Ultra Essentiel, Ultra). The freedom to leave does not excuse you from reading the "then X euros per month" line.

## With or without a contract: what you really lose

The 12-month commitment at Orange and Bouygues Telecom does not come with a lower price over time: the **Bbox Fit** at 30.99 euros then 37.99 euros and the **Livebox Fibre** cost more over 24 months than the Freebox Pop S. What you buy with the commitment is access to the operator's fixed network and its associated services (multiple repeaters at Orange, mobile discounts at Bouygues). What you lose is the ability to leave if customer service disappoints, whereas the ARCEP 2025 observatory precisely reports a decline for Bouygues Telecom on fixed-line problem resolution (78%).
