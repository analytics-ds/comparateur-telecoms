> **In brief:**
> 1. Free is the best French operator on value for money in 2026: contract-free everywhere, fixed or frozen prices, and the top overall satisfaction score in the ARCEP observatory (8.1 out of 10).
> 2. Orange keeps the best mobile network as measured by ARCEP (91% of perfect calls) and charges more for it, with a 12-month commitment on its Livebox offers.
> 3. Bouygues Telecom tops the nPerf 2026 barometer on latency, SFR remains the lowest-rated operator on satisfaction (7.5 out of 10).

Four operators own a network in France: **Orange**, **SFR**, **Bouygues Telecom** and **Free**. Every other brand (Sosh, RED by SFR, B&You, La Poste Mobile, Prixtel...) rents one of these four networks. Choosing the best telecom operator therefore means choosing between four networks, four pricing policies and four customer services. Here is the 2026 comparison, built on the operators' public price lists recorded on 13 September 2026 and on the measurements published by ARCEP and nPerf.

## 1. Free, the best value for money

**Free** is the only one of the four operators to sell **contract-free** offers exclusively, on boxes as on mobile. That is the first reason for its ranking: subscribers can leave the following month if the service does not suit them, which forces the operator to hold its prices.

On boxes, the **Freebox Pop S** at 24.99 euros per month is the only fibre offer on the market whose price does not move after one year. Every competitor applies an increase in month 13, often of 7 to 10 euros. The range then climbs with the Freebox Pop (29.99 euros then 39.99 euros), the Ultra Essentiel (39.99 euros then 49.99 euros) and the **Freebox Ultra** (49.99 euros then 59.99 euros), which includes Netflix, Disney+ and Prime Video with quad-band Wi-Fi 7 and symmetrical 8 Gbit/s.

On mobile, the **Free 5G+ 350 GB plan** at 19.99 euros per month is sold with a price frozen until 2027, and the **2 euro plan** (2 hours of calls, 50 MB) drops to 0 euro for Freebox subscribers. The new **Free Max plan** (unlimited 5G, unlimited data in more than 135 destinations) costs 29.99 euros, reduced to 19.99 euros for Freebox subscribers.

The ARCEP customer satisfaction observatory published in 2025 gives Free the **best overall score**, 8.1 out of 10, ahead of Orange (8.0), Bouygues Telecom (7.8) and SFR (7.5). ARCEP also notes that Free Mobile is the operator reporting the fewest problems.

The weak point is known: on call quality measured by ARCEP in 2025, Free is fourth with 84% of perfect calls, and 76% in rural areas. The nPerf 2026 barometer stresses, on the other hand, that Free offers the **widest 5G coverage**, carried by the 700 MHz band.

## 2. Orange, the best network at a premium price

**Orange** is crowned best mobile network by ARCEP for the fifteenth consecutive year, with 91% of perfect calls nationally, 83% in rural areas and 74% of calls maintained on long-distance trains. On speeds, nPerf measures the best average download speed in 2026 (128.91 Mbit/s).

That quality comes at a price. The **Livebox Max Fibre** costs 47.99 euros per month for 12 months then 57.99 euros, with a 12-month commitment and 49 euros of set-up fees (waived in a promotion until 7 October 2026). It carries symmetrical 8 Gbit/s fibre, Wi-Fi 7 and up to three repeaters. The low-cost brand **Sosh** gives contract-free access to the Orange network, for example with a 200 GB 5G Voyage plan at 15.99 euros per month, more generous than average on international use.

On satisfaction, Orange is first on fixed-line problem resolution (90%) and on speed of handling (78% of incidents resolved in under three days).

## 3. Bouygues Telecom, the fastest network day to day

**Bouygues Telecom** tops the nPerf 2026 barometer with an overall score of 95,684 points: best latency (30.15 ms), best web browsing and best 5G speed (231 Mbit/s on the 3.5 GHz band). In dense urban areas, ARCEP puts it level with Orange, above 95% call quality.

On offers, the **Bbox Fit** fibre costs 30.99 euros per month for 12 months then 37.99 euros, the **Bbox Must** 36.99 euros then 43.99 euros, and the **Bbox Ultym** 45.99 euros with a Bouygues mobile plan (52.99 euros without). All carry a commitment. The **B&You** brand is the only one to offer 5G from 9.99 euros per month, contract-free.

The 2025 weak point is customer service: ARCEP notes a marked decline in fixed-line problem resolution (78%) and the slowest handling (54% within three days).

## 4. SFR, the widest range but the lowest rated

**SFR** offers the most tiered fibre range: Box Starter at 27.99 euros, Power S at 29.99 euros, Power at 36.99 euros and Premium at 45.99 euros per month, the latter being the only one without a contract. The **RED by SFR** brand remains one of the simplest offers on the market: a fibre box at 22.99 euros per month with no contract, a 150 GB 5G plan at 11.99 euros with no scheduled increase.

The network is improving: ARCEP measures 87% of perfect calls in 2025, and SFR ranks first on mobile problem resolution (88%). But overall satisfaction remains the lowest of the four (7.5 out of 10), which puts it last.

## How to choose by profile

- **Tight budget and zero commitment**: Free (Freebox Pop S, 2 euro plan or Série Free) or RED by SFR.
- **Mobile network first, in rural areas or on trains**: Orange, or Sosh for the same network without a contract.
- **Heavy data user in the city**: Bouygues Telecom or B&You, best on latency and 5G.
- **Household wanting everything included**: Freebox Ultra (Netflix, Disney+, Prime Video included) or Livebox Max (discounts on four platforms).
- **Family with several lines**: Free, with the free 2 euro plan and up to 10 euros off per plan for Freebox subscribers.

## Method and sources

Prices quoted are those publicly displayed by the operators and recorded on 13 September 2026; they change frequently and deserve a check before subscribing. Network quality data comes from the [ARCEP 2025 measurement campaign](https://www.arcep.fr/actualites/actualites-et-communiques/detail/n/qualite-des-services-mobiles-en-metropole-201125.html) (more than one million measurements between June and August) and from the [nPerf mobile connections barometer](https://media.nperf.com/files/publications/FR/FR-Barometer-mobile-connections-nPerf-2025_7885.pdf). Satisfaction data comes from the [ARCEP customer satisfaction observatory](https://www.arcep.fr/cartes-et-donnees/nos-publications-chiffrees/satisfaction-client/observatoire-de-la-satisfaction-client-edition-2026.html).
